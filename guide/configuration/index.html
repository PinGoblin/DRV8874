    Configuration: PMODE jumper (JP1)¶

    JP1 is a 3-pad solder jumper that sets the control mode for both drivers. It ships with pads 1–2 bridged (PH/EN mode).

    Warning

    Never leave all three pads open: that selects a half-bridge mode this board isn't designed for.

    JP1 setting Mode J3 pin 3 / 5 J3 pin 4 / 6 Test buttons Library
    1–2 bridged (default) PH/EN EN: on/off + PWM speed PH: direction Work mode="ph_en"
    2–3 bridged PWM (IN1/IN2) IN1 IN2 Do not use mode="pwm"

    To change it, cut the thin trace between pads 1 and 2 with a craft knife, check with a multimeter that they're open, then bridge pads 2 and 3 with solder.

    PWM mode truth table¶

    Motor A; motor B is the same.

    IN1 (pin 3) IN2 (pin 4) OUT1 OUT2 Motor
    Low Low Open Open Coast
    High Low High Low Forward
    Low High Low High Reverse
    High High Low Low Brake

    PWM mode suits libraries written for two-input drivers such as the L298N or DRV8833 (gpiozero Motor, Arduino analogWrite on either pin). With gpiozero's Motor class, pass forward= J3 pin 3 (IN1) and backward= J3 pin 4 (IN2) so its directions match the table above.

    In PWM mode each motor can coast on its own, without sleeping the board.

    Speed control in each mode¶

    In both modes, speed comes from the PWM duty cycle: the fraction of each PWM period for which the motor is connected to the supply. A motor driven at 60% duty sees, on average, about 60% of VM, so it runs at roughly 60% of its full speed at that load. What differs between the modes is which pin you switch, and what the motor does in the off-time.

    Mode Forward at speed d Reverse at speed d Off-time Response
    PH/EN (default) PH high, EN PWM at duty d PH low, EN PWM at duty d Brake Firm, close to linear
    PWM, slow decay (what the library uses) IN1 high, IN2 PWM at duty 1 − d IN2 high, IN1 PWM at duty 1 − d Brake The same as PH/EN
    PWM, fast decay IN1 PWM at duty d, IN2 low IN2 PWM at duty d, IN1 low Coast Softer; speed falls away at low duty

    PH/EN mode. EN carries the speed and PH the direction, so a single PWM channel per motor is enough. During the off-time both low-side switches are on (brake, also called slow decay), so the motor's current keeps circulating and the speed follows the duty cycle closely, even at light load. This is the easiest mode to use and gives the best low-speed control. The library's speed_pc (0–100) is the EN duty cycle.

    PWM mode, slow decay. One input is held high and the other is switched. The motor is driven while the switched input is low, so its duty is inverted: for 30% speed forward, hold IN1 high and run IN2 at 70%. The off-time is a brake, so the response matches PH/EN mode. The library does this inversion for you in mode="pwm".

    PWM mode, fast decay. One input is held low and the other is switched at the speed duty, which is how most two-input motor libraries work, including gpiozero's Motor class and Arduino code that calls analogWrite() on one pin. In the off-time both outputs turn off and the motor coasts, while its current flows back into the supply. Expect less torque and a less linear response at low duty: a lightly loaded motor may barely slow until the duty is well below 50%. It works, but slow decay gives finer control.

    Practical limits¶

    • Minimum speed. Most small motors don't start turning until the duty cycle reaches about 10–30%, because of friction and gearbox losses. Below that they hum without turning. The library's min_speed_pc raises any non-zero speed to this value.
    • PWM frequency. Use 20 kHz where the host supports it: it's above human hearing and gives smooth current. Lower frequencies (1–5 kHz, such as the Pi's software PWM) work the same way but make the motor whine.
    • Full speed. 100% duty applies the full supply to the motor. The test buttons drive EN high, so they always run at 100%.

    100% duty and the current limit

    The drivers limit current on every PWM cycle and restart on the next PWM edge. At exactly 100% duty there are no edges, so if a motor stalls and reaches the current limit, the driver holds it in brake (and lights the fault LED) until the input changes. To recover, change the speed, stop and restart, or release and press the test button again. If a stall is likely at full speed, run at 99% instead: the motor is effectively at full speed, and the current limit then recovers on its own.
